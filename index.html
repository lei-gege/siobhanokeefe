Biography

Caring for the sickest children, and for their families.

Dr O'Keefe's clinical work and research share one aim: that every critically ill child receives the right care, including palliative care, when they need it.

Dr Siobhán O'Keefe is a consultant in Intensive Care Medicine at Children's Health Ireland (CHI) at Temple Street, part of CHI's national Critical Care Medicine service. She cares for children with life-threatening illness and injury, and for children recovering from major surgery.

She qualified in medicine (MB BCh BAO) at the Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland. She then trained in combined Internal Medicine–Pediatrics at the University of Michigan, and served as Assistant Professor at the Brody School of Medicine, East Carolina University, teaching in the Med-Peds residency programme. She later trained in Pediatric Critical Care at Children's Hospital Colorado and the University of Colorado School of Medicine, before returning to Ireland to join CHI.

Her research uses large multicentre datasets to study how palliative care reaches critically ill children, and where access is unequal. She also contributes to the national Irish Paediatric Critical Care Audit.

Research

Palliative care for critically ill children

Multicentre health-services research using the Pediatric Health Information System (PHIS), carried out with Children's Hospital Colorado and the University of Colorado.

Pediatric Critical Care Medicine · 2021

Variation in Pediatric Palliative Care Allocation Among Critically Ill Children in the United States

Across more than one million ICU hospitalisations at 52 children's hospitals from 2007 to 2018, palliative care consultation increased over time but varied widely between institutions. This suggests children do not have equal access to palliative care.

Hospital Pediatrics · 2021

Measuring palliative care in large datasets

A validation study of 4,670 PICU hospitalisations. It found that administrative codes for palliative care are highly specific but miss most real consultations, which matters for any research that relies on these codes.
